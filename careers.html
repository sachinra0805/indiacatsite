Career ladder

Where the path leads

Real trajectories from a decade of watching analysts grow into industry leaders.

①

Analyst (0–2 yrs)

Run models, cleanse exposure data, learn the platforms. Master the four modules and one vendor platform deeply. Python on the side compounds fast.

②

Senior analyst / Validator (2–5 yrs)

Own model validation, sensitivity testing, and underwriter communication. The analysts who can explain a loss number get promoted.

③

Lead / CoE leadership (5+ yrs)

Run teams, set modeling strategy, and bridge the CoE with underwriting, capital, and C-suite. Vendor relationships and model governance live here.

Lateral moves

Cat modeling opens doors: underwriting, capital & reserving, climate risk, product management at model vendors, and data science roles that value domain depth.

The India advantage

India hosts CoEs for global insurers, reinsurers, brokers, and Big Four consulting. Demand for experienced modelers consistently outstrips supply — especially validators and leads.

What employers look for

The skill stack

  • Four-module fluency — hazard, exposure, vulnerability, financial model
  • One vendor platform, deeply — Touchstone or RMS in production
  • Python — pandas, NumPy, geospatial basics
  • Exposure data craft — geocoding, cleansing, portfolio QA
  • Communication — explaining loss numbers to non-modelers
  • Peril curiosity — read event reports, follow the science

Interview edge

Hiring managers test three things: can you run the model, can you validate the output, and can you explain it. Walk in with a portfolio — a cleaned exposure dataset, a validation notebook, a written model review — and you stand out from every resume in the pile.
